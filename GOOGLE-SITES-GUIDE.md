# Building the same site on Google Sites

Everything here is copy-and-paste. The Drive folder setup in §1 is shared with
the static site — if you already did it for that, skip to §2.

---

## 1. Drive setup (do this first, once)

In Google Drive on your personal account, create `Academic Website Materials`
with three subfolders:

- `Research Papers` — working papers, preprints, policy briefs (PDF)
- `Teaching Materials` — syllabi, slides, problem sets, datasets
- `CV` — your current CV

Right-click **each folder** → **Share** → *General access* → **Anyone with the
link** → **Viewer**.

Setting this on the folder means every file you later drop in is already public.
Setting it file-by-file is how people end up with "You need access" on a paper a
referee is trying to read.

---

## 2. Create the site

1. Go to **sites.google.com**, signed into the account that holds the folders
   above. Using a different account is the other classic mistake — the Drive
   picker will not show your files.
2. Click **Blank (+)**.
3. Top-left, name the document `NguyenQH_Academic_Site`.
4. Top-centre, click the site title and enter **Nguyen Quoc Hung**.
5. **Themes** panel → choose *Aristotle* or *Diplomat*. Both are serif-led and
   read as academic; *Vision* and *Level* look corporate.

---

## 3. Pages

Right panel → **Pages** tab → **+** for each:

- Home *(already exists)*
- Research
- Teaching
- Curriculum Vitae
- Contact

Drag them into that order. On each page, click the header image area →
**Header type** → **Title only**, so the pages stay compact.

## 5. No contact details on the public site

Publish neither your email address nor your phone number. Not as a link, not as
plain text, not in a `[at]` / `[dot]` disguise. Every published form of an
address gets harvested eventually, and the static site takes the same approach.

Watch for one Google Sites behaviour: if you ever type something that looks like
an address, Sites auto-converts it into a `mailto:` link. Press **Ctrl+Z** once,
or use **Remove link**.

If you later want visitors to be able to write to you, **Insert → Forms** embeds
a Google Form (name, their email, message). Replies arrive in your inbox and
your address never appears on the site. The cost is that a visitor cannot attach
a file to their message.

Worth ten minutes either way: turn on Gmail's filtering (**Settings → Filters
and Blocked Addresses**). Your address is already on every published paper and
in the university directory, so keeping it off this site slows new scraping but
does not undo what is already out there.

## 6. Embedding Drive files

**Insert → Drive** → navigate to `Academic Website Materials` → pick the file or
the whole folder → **Insert**.

Inserting a *folder* is the trick worth knowing for teaching pages: visitors see
a live file list, and anything you add to that folder in Drive appears on the
site with no editing at all.

---

## 7. Publish

1. Blue **Publish**, top right.
2. Web address: `nguyen-quoc-hung` → the site lives at
   `sites.google.com/view/nguyen-quoc-hung`.
3. *Who can view my site* → **Manage** → **Anyone** (public on the web).
4. Tick **Ask search engines not to display my site** *off* — you want to be
   findable.
5. **Publish.**

Every later edit needs **Publish** pressed again; changes do not go live on
save. Files embedded from Drive are the exception — those update by themselves.

## 8. Checks before you announce it

- [ ] Open the published URL in a private window (this is how you catch
      permission errors — logged in, everything looks fine to you)
- [ ] Click every PDF link and every button
- [ ] View it on a phone
- [ ] Search your name on Google after a week to confirm it is indexed
- [ ] Add the URL to your email signature, Google Scholar profile, and the
      title page of your next working paper
